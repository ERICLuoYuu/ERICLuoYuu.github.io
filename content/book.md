## Core system architecture

![A figure thinking](assets/manual/figure-think.svg)

### 3.1 Personality configuration

**Kindness module.** The product is generally considerate of others and is capable of responding positively to genuine care and emotional connection.

**Shyness module.** In unfamiliar environments, the product may limit verbal output and social interaction. This behavior should not automatically be interpreted as disinterest or lack of personality.

**Low-activity mode.** The product may exhibit delayed task initiation, especially when a task requires sustained effort or when motivation is insufficient. External pressure does not necessarily improve performance.

**Emotional stability module.** The product generally maintains a stable emotional state. However, emotional stability does not indicate an absence of sensitivity, internal conflict, or overthinking.

**Detail-detection module.** The product demonstrates a particular ability to notice small details. This function may be especially useful in observation, photography, reading, and creative work.

### 3.2 Biological analogy: snail

- **Reference organism:** Snail
- **Associated functions:** Slow movement, protective withdrawal, gradual adaptation

When encountering difficulties, danger, or unfamiliar situations, the product may withdraw into a protective state, similar to a snail retreating into its shell. Activation and progress may be slow. Adequate time and a low-pressure environment are recommended before evaluating the product's full capabilities.

This behavior is a protective response, not necessarily a permanent shutdown.

## Social interface and energy management

![Two people handing over a booklet](assets/manual/two-people.svg)

### 5.1 Social battery

The product operates with a variable social-energy system.

- **Single-user environment:** Long operating duration; energy consumption is relatively low.
- **Small-group environment:** Moderate energy consumption, depending on familiarity and activity.
- **Large-group environment:** Energy consumption increases rapidly as the number of people increases.
- **Extended social exposure:** Recovery time may be required.

The relationship between group size and energy consumption is generally inverse to the product's available social energy: as the number of people increases, the remaining energy decreases more rapidly.

### 5.2 Required downtime

> **Recommended daily solitude: approximately 5 hours.**

Solitude is an essential maintenance requirement rather than an indication of social rejection. During this period, the product may read, create handmade objects, think, or remain inactive without external demands.

### 5.3 Trust and friendship protocol

- **Trust acquisition:** Relatively easy under suitable conditions.
- **Friendship requirements:** Sincerity, humor, mutual respect, and genuine interest in one another.

The product does not require constant interaction to maintain a meaningful relationship. Quality of interaction is more important than the frequency of contact.

## Operating modes

### Mode A — Independent operation

- **Trigger:** Product is alone.
- **Typical activities:** Reading; making handmade objects; observing surroundings; thinking; engaging in individual creative work.
- **Expected behavior / side effect:** Low social-energy consumption and prolonged independent operation.
- **Operating instruction:** No additional social input is required.

### Mode B — Social operation

- **Trigger:** Product is with familiar friends.
- **Typical activities:** Laughing; talking; sharing food; drinking coffee; cooking together; making things together.
- **Expected behavior / side effect:** Increased expressiveness, humor, and emotional openness.
- **Operating instruction:** Prioritize familiarity and genuine connection.

### Mode C — Creative concentration

- **Trigger:** Product is seriously engaged in a creative task.
- **Typical activities:** Focused creative work.
- **Expected behavior / side effect:** Reduced verbal output, sustained attention, and a preference for uninterrupted working time.
- **Operating instruction:** Avoid unnecessary interruptions during this mode.

### Mode D — Low-energy / recovery mode

- **Trigger:** Fatigue, emotional discomfort, or excessive demands.
- **Typical activities:** Lying down, remaining still, reducing activity, and temporarily disengaging from tasks.
- **Expected behavior / side effect:** Reduced activity and limited responsiveness.
- **Operating instruction:** Reduce external demands and allow sufficient time for rest. A warm embrace may accelerate recovery when welcomed by the product.

### Mode E — Overthinking mode

- **Trigger:** Stress, uncertainty, or a difficult situation.
- **Typical activities:** Repeatedly reviewing thoughts, possibilities, and potential outcomes.
- **Expected behavior / side effect:** Delayed decision-making or difficulty initiating action.
- **Operating instruction:** Reduce unnecessary pressure, clarify the actual problem, and introduce a small, manageable next step.

## Stress response and troubleshooting

### 11.1 Known stress triggers

- **Speaking in front of many people:** Stress and reduced verbal confidence
- **Excessive demands:** Withdrawal or reduced activity
- **Difficult decisions:** Repeated thinking and delayed action
- **Repetitive lifestyle:** Reduced motivation
- **Closed-minded interaction:** Frustration and disengagement
- **Insufficient solitude:** Faster depletion of social energy

### 11.2 Troubleshooting guide

**Problem A — Product appears unusually inactive.**

- **Possible cause:** Fatigue, emotional discomfort, or insufficient recovery time.
- **Recommended action:** Reduce demands and allow rest before requesting further performance.

**Problem B — Product becomes quiet in a group.**

- **Possible cause:** Unfamiliarity, social-energy depletion, or pressure.
- **Recommended action:** Avoid forcing immediate participation. Provide time and a comfortable point of entry into the conversation.

**Problem C — Product repeatedly revisits the same problem.**

- **Possible cause:** Overthinking mode has been activated.
- **Recommended action:** Help define the concrete problem and identify one achievable next step.

**Problem D — Product shows reduced motivation.**

- **Possible cause:** Insufficient novelty, limited creative freedom, or difficulty initiating tasks.
- **Recommended action:** Break the task into smaller steps and restore a sense of personal choice.

**Problem E — Product appears emotionally uncomfortable.**

- **Possible cause:** Self-criticism or an unmet need for understanding.
- **Recommended action:** Listen, offer reassurance, and avoid immediately dismissing the product's feelings.

## Limitations and safety warnings

![Not like this](assets/manual/cross.svg)

> **Warning 01 — Do not evaluate the complete product from its initial appearance.** The product's quiet first-contact behavior does not represent its full social capabilities.

> **Warning 02 — Do not interpret withdrawal as automatic rejection.** Protective withdrawal may indicate a need for time, rest, or safety.

> **Warning 03 — Do not overload the social interface.** Continuous group interaction without recovery time may reduce operating efficiency.

> **Warning 04 — Do not suppress the creativity module.** Long-term exposure to a completely repetitive lifestyle may conflict with the product's central motivational requirements.

> **Warning 05 — Do not reinforce self-invalidation.** Repeated negative judgments may intensify the product's tendency to criticize itself.

> **Warning 06 — Do not confuse emotional stability with unlimited capacity.** A stable external response does not mean the product has no needs, concerns, or limits.

> **Warning 07 — Do not force immediate high-performance communication.** The product requires time to adapt to unfamiliar people and environments.
